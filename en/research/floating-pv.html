Floating solar PV for sheltered waters

Floating PV makes use of the surface of reservoirs, lakes and sheltered coastal waters. The challenge lies beneath the panels: the floats and mooring lines must keep the array stable in waves. This project studies float shape, orientation and mooring through model tests and programs developed at MARDEV.

Model tests in the wave flume

A barge-type floating PV model with four mooring lines was tested in the BRIN wave flume in Yogyakarta in May 2026. Tests like this show how the array moves and how the mooring lines are loaded in controlled waves, as a check on the program results.

Two float concepts in irregular waves

These program animations compare two 3×2 float arrays, 3.8–3.9 m long: a box pontoon and a catamaran. Both are slack-moored in 7 m of water and run in irregular waves with a significant wave height of 0.6 m and a peak period of 3.1 s.

In other words, how the array faces the dominant waves matters as much as the choice of float. Results like these set the layout, operating limits and mooring of a floating PV plant before it is built.

What comes next

The work continues towards multi-use floating platforms that combine solar panels with wave or wind energy, including wave loads on multi-body structures and the design of their moorings. This topic is open to undergraduate, master’s and PhD students.
