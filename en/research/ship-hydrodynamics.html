Ship hydrodynamics and seakeeping in real seas

Ships rarely sail in calm water. This research looks at what happens when a ship meets waves: the loads on the hull, its motions and safety, and the extra power it needs. It grew out of a doctoral thesis at Osaka University on wave loads from pressures measured on the hull, and is now supported by an in-house hydrodynamics program checked against model-test data.

Four research themes

01

Wave loads & hull strength

Vertical bending moment and wave loads estimated from pressure distributions measured on the model hull. This was the doctoral topic at Osaka University, later developed into a wave-load program for longitudinal hull strength.

02

Ship motions & routing

Realistic ship motion simulation in rough seas as the basis for safe, fuel-efficient routing, including safety evaluation of ships at anchor in open waters.

03

Damaged-ferry seakeeping

Motions of passenger ferries intact and after flooding, with damage scenarios from probabilistic stability analysis.

04

Resistance & propulsion

Drag reduction and energy efficiency: energy-saving fins on a 17,500 DWT tanker, multihulls (trimaran and pentamaran), microbubble injection, and hydrofoils.

What comes next

The program is being extended for two needs of the maritime industry in the coming years: autonomous ships, with motion and environmental load prediction as the basis for control and route planning of uncrewed vessels, and low-emission ship design, assessing hull form, wind assistance and new fuels from the earliest design stage.
