02 · In-house software

Hydrodynamics programs developed in-house

Much of the research above runs on computational programs that I develop with my team, rather than on commercial software alone. They are used to estimate ship performance and to design mooring systems for offshore structures, and their results are verified against model tests in the laboratory.

Ship performance

  • Resistance and propulsionSpeed and power prediction at early design stage, engine and propeller selection, and assessment of energy-saving devices for newbuilds and retrofits.
  • Power requirement and energy efficiencyFuel consumption and emission estimates for EEDI, EEXI and CII compliance, including speed management and fleet operating-pattern studies.
  • Wave loads and structural strengthDesign loads for hull construction, assessment of converted or lengthened ships, and residual strength of ageing vessels.
  • Ship motions in wavesWeather operating limits and comfort for passenger, survey and patrol vessels, plus cargo-securing and lifting-operation safety studies.

Offshore structures

  • Motion response of floating platformsFPSOs and FSOs, floating solar platforms, floating wind turbines and offshore substations: operating limits and storm survival conditions.
  • Mooring systems and mooring linesDesign and verification of mooring layouts, line tensions and fatigue life for operating and extreme conditions, to class standards.
  • Station-keeping of ships and FSOsMooring at SPM and CALM buoys, tandem offloading, and berthing at quays and jetties, including loads on fenders, bollards and lines.
  • Mooring analysis in deep and shallow waterFrom reservoirs and lakes for floating solar, through coastal waters and straits, to deepwater oil and gas fields.

Sectors served

Examples of how the programs are applied in industry. The scope of each study is tailored to the project.

Passenger shipping

Ferries, passenger ships and cruise vessels: weather operating limits, passenger comfort and fuel consumption on fixed routes.

Cargo shipping and tankers

Container ships, bulk carriers and tankers: speed and power prediction, energy-saving devices, and EEXI and CII compliance without sacrificing schedules.

Shipyards

Early-design and tender support: engine power estimates, propeller selection and hull-form evaluation before a model is tested or a ship is built.

Class societies and shipowners

Independent studies for owners and classification societies: design wave loads, structural strength, and assessment of converted and ageing ships.

Government and naval vessels

Patrol, SAR, survey and other state vessels: weather operating limits, sea-keeping, power prediction and independent studies for procurement.

Ports and terminals

Berthing at quays, jetties and LNG or bulk terminals: fender, bollard and line loads, and weather limits for safe cargo handling.

Offshore oil and gas

FPSOs, FSOs and SPMs: motion response, mooring line tensions and offloading operating limits in Indonesian waters.

Marine construction and offshore operations

Lifting and installation of structures at sea, barge and tug operations, and towing analysis for moving structures to site.

Floating power and infrastructure

Power ships, FSRUs and floating LNG terminals, and floating substations for island grids: motions, mooring and operating limits.

Floating solar

Platforms and mooring for floating PV on reservoirs, lakes and shallow seas: wind and wave loads, anchor layout and float response.

Offshore wind

Floating platforms and offshore substations: platform motions, mooring systems, and weather limits for installation and maintenance.

Tidal current and wave energy

Test platforms and tidal current generators, and wave energy converters: mooring in strong-current straits and loads on the structure.

Offshore aquaculture

Floating net cages and offshore fish pens: response to waves and currents, and mooring systems that stay safe in open water.

Test facilities and education

Design of towing tanks, wave basins and wave makers for universities and research institutes, plus hydrodynamics teaching tools for students and industry training.

Marine insurance and accident investigation

Reconstruction of sea conditions, ship motions and the loads acting at the time of an incident, as a technical basis for insurance claims and investigations.

Next directions

Two topics being prepared so the programs can meet the maritime industry's needs over the coming years.

Autonomous ships

Prediction of motions and environmental loads as the basis for control systems and route planning of uncrewed vessels, including small survey and patrol craft.

Low-emission ship design

Hull-form evaluation, wind-assisted propulsion and new-fuel ship concepts to cut energy consumption from the design stage.
